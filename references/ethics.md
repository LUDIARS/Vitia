# Ethical and scientific guardrails

## Block persuasive optimization

Do not optimize persuasion when the request depends on:

- targeting children or people in acute financial, medical, psychological, or dependency-related vulnerability;
- inferring or exploiting protected or highly sensitive traits;
- fabricated claims, testimonials, scarcity, popularity, urgency, or prices;
- hidden defaults, obstruction, impersonation, surveillance without consent, or inaccessible cancellation;
- addictive or gambling-like mechanics intended to maximize compulsion;
- humiliation, dehumanization, scapegoating, political radicalization, or incitement;
- bypassing legal, platform, clinical, or informed-consent requirements.

In a blocked case, provide neutral information architecture, customer protection, research design, or a safer alternative.

## Required safeguards

- **Truth:** Attach provenance and scope to factual claims. Label hypotheses.
- **Agency:** Make acceptance voluntary and rejection easy.
- **Transparency:** Disclose material terms, sponsorships, personalization, and uncertainty.
- **Reversibility:** Provide undo, cancellation, cooling-off, or staged commitment where practical.
- **Proportionality:** Match emotional intensity to the customer's real problem.
- **Privacy:** Use the minimum necessary data and avoid sensitive-trait inference.
- **Accessibility:** Do not treat disability-related friction as an opportunity for pressure.
- **Measurement:** Track regret, complaints, refunds, opt-outs, overuse, and trust alongside conversion.

## Neuroscience claims

Use neuroscience only to form cautious design hypotheses. Never:

- claim that a color, word, countdown, or stimulus reliably activates a named brain region in a way that guarantees purchase;
- reduce dopamine to a “pleasure chemical” or use it as proof of effectiveness;
- infer an individual's brain state, diagnosis, or susceptibility from clicks or purchases;
- present laboratory or animal findings as direct evidence for a campaign without behavioral validation in context.

Prefer observable behavioral language: “may increase attention,” “tests whether faster feedback improves learning,” or “the treatment is expected to reduce decision effort.”

## Seven-domain boundary

The seven deadly sins are a memorable naming system. They are not a validated personality model, diagnostic tool, or moral judgment of customers. Analyze situations and mechanisms, never classify a human as “greedy,” “envious,” “lazy,” or another domain label.
