# Evidence base and interpretation limits

## Contents

1. General decision and identity mechanisms

Prefer the linked primary articles when making a strong scientific claim. The domain mapping is a Vitia design convention; the papers validate component mechanisms, not the seven-domain system as a whole.

## 1. General decision and identity mechanisms

- Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1979). [Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185). Reference-dependent value, gains and losses, and probability weighting. Relevant to Avaritia.
- Berger, J., & Heath, C. (2007). [Where Consumers Diverge from Others: Identity Signaling and Product Domains](https://doi.org/10.1086/519142). Product choices can communicate desired or undesired identities in identity-relevant domains. Relevant to Superbia.
- Van de Ven, N., Zeelenberg, M., & Pieters, R. (2011). [The Envy Premium in Product Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1086/657239). Distinguishes approach effects associated with benign envy from differentiation effects associated with malicious envy. Relevant to Invidia.

## 2. Domain-specific evidence

- Pessiglione, M. et al. (2006). [Dopamine-dependent prediction errors underpin reward-seeking behaviour in humans](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05051). Supports a role for prediction-error learning in human choice; it does not justify “dopamine button” marketing claims. Relevant to Gula.
- Hollerman, J. R., & Schultz, W. (1998). [Dopamine neurons report an error in the temporal prediction of reward during learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/1124). Foundational reward-prediction-error evidence in nonhuman primates; apply to consumer design only as an indirect hypothesis. Relevant to Gula.
- Inman, J. J. (2001). [The Role of Sensory-Specific Satiety in Attribute-Level Variety Seeking](https://doi.org/10.1086/321950). Connects sensory-specific satiation with variety seeking across consumer data and studies. Relevant to Gula and Luxuria.
- McAlister, L. (1982). [A Dynamic Attribute Satiation Model of Variety-Seeking Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1086/208907). Models choice as dependent on consumption history and attribute satiation. Relevant to Gula.
- Bertini, M., & Aydinli, A. (2020). [Consumer Reactance to Promotional Favors](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2020.03.002). Experiments show that some action-contingent promotional favors can evoke reactance and reduce spending. Relevant to Ira.
- Wang, G., Wang, Y., & Gai, X. (2021). [A Meta-Analysis of the Effects of Mental Contrasting With Implementation Intentions on Goal Attainment](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202). Finds a small-to-medium aggregate effect with limitations including publication bias. Relevant to Acedia.

## 3. Interpretation rules

1. Distinguish mechanism evidence from campaign evidence. A mechanism paper does not prove a specific headline or UI treatment works.
2. Prefer behavioral outcomes over neural proxies for campaign decisions.
3. State the population, context, intervention, comparator, and outcome before generalizing.
4. Treat effect direction and boundary conditions as more portable than exact effect size.
5. Mark evidence as direct, adjacent, or speculative for the actual offer.
6. Require an experiment or strong quasi-experimental design before claiming campaign causality.
7. Update this file when an algorithm changes materially; do not silently cite a theory for a tactic it does not test.
