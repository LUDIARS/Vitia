## 1. Governing objective

Optimize durable value exchange, informed choice, and revenue quality—not gross
spend in isolation. A monetization model is neither ethical nor exploitative by
name. Apply the same evidence standard to premium purchase, subscription, DLC,
advertising, cosmetic sales, consumables, and randomized rewards.

Replace the model and product names with neutral IDs. If the judgment changes
without a change in mechanics, price, outcomes, agency, or risk, repeat the
audit. Source phrases such as “human bugs” and “taking money” describe a framing
to examine; they are not scientific mechanisms or permission to exploit users.

## 2. Monetization truth ledger

Record before selecting a Vitia domain:

- customer segment and age boundary;
- value received before, at, and after payment;
- real-money price, virtual-currency conversion, fees, tax, cadence, renewal,
  expiry, and maximum plausible cost;
- ownership, access duration, portability, refund, cancellation, and what is
  lost on exit;
- randomness, published probabilities, duplicates, pity or guarantee rules,
  inventory limits, and resale or cash-out;
- purchase trigger, including whether it follows failure, time pressure, social
  comparison, depleted resources, or a deliberately introduced obstruction;
- proof that the purchased benefit is delivered;
- who bears downside risk and how the user can recover.

Unknown cost or outcome is not zero. Resolve it or mark the assessment
exploratory.

## 3. Model-neutral audit

### Value legibility

The user must be able to understand the promised value before commitment. For a
premium product this may require a truthful demo, evidence, scope, and refund
terms; for an in-product purchase it may require experiencing the core loop
without a paid obstruction. “Value before ask” does not require giving every
product away for free.

### Cost legibility

Show the total real-money equivalent and material recurrence before purchase.
Do not make virtual currency, bundles, temporary discounts, or multiple exchange
steps obscure comparison. Disclose expiry, auto-renewal, probabilities, and
material exclusions at the decision point.

### Agency and exit

Buying, declining, cancelling, setting limits, and leaving must be usable and
accessible. Confirm actions in proportion to cost and irreversibility. Preserve
receipts, purchase history, restore-purchase behavior, and a meaningful remedy.

### Timing and pressure

Do not place the offer primarily where frustration, loss chasing, fatigue,
social humiliation, or a blocked goal reduces deliberation. Scarcity and urgency
must be real, material, and disclosed. A paid shortcut must not merely remove
avoidable pain introduced to sell the shortcut.

### Customer-outcome integrity

Verify that spend improves a customer outcome rather than only increasing time,
dependency, or the next purchase opportunity. Separate desired collection or
competition from pressure, pay-to-keep-up, and loss of previously earned value.

## 4. Model-specific questions

### Premium purchase and DLC

- Is scope and platform compatibility clear before purchase?
- Does later DLC preserve the completeness of the represented base product?
- Are ownership, offline access, support horizon, and refund boundaries clear?

### Subscription and season access

- Are billing cadence, renewal date, price changes, and unused benefits visible?
- Is cancellation comparable in effort to sign-up?
- What happens to content, progress, data, and paid currency after cancellation?

### Direct in-game items and consumables

- Does the item deliver durable value or only remove manufactured obstruction?
- Can competition, social obligation, or repeated depletion create coercive
  spend pressure?
- Are purchase limits and the cumulative real-money total visible?

### Paid randomized rewards

- Block child-directed or vulnerability-targeted optimization.
- Show probabilities, pool, duplicates, guarantees, reset rules, and real-money
  cost before purchase.
- Provide spending history, limits, cooling-off, and non-random alternatives.
- Treat loss chasing, near misses, variable-ratio compulsion, and effectively
  unbounded spend as blocking risks rather than optimization opportunities.

Observed associations between loot-box spending and problem-gambling severity
do not establish individual diagnosis or causal direction. They do justify a
high-risk classification and stronger protection.

### Advertising and rewarded advertising

- Distinguish optional reward from forced interruption.
- State the reward and duration accurately and make close controls legible.
- Measure accidental clicks, fatigue, privacy, and the effect on core value.

## 5. Revenue-quality metrics

Report revenue with customer and risk outcomes over an appropriate horizon:

- net revenue after refunds, chargebacks, platform fees, and support cost;
- payer conversion and retention without using ARPPU alone as success;
- distribution and concentration of spend, including extreme-tail exposure;
- refund, accidental purchase, complaint, cancellation, payment failure, and
  support-contact rates;
- time to first value, value-qualified retention, trust, regret, overuse, and
  realized customer benefit;
- accessibility failures and differences across predefined non-vulnerable
  segments.

A treatment fails when revenue rises by increasing unacceptable regret,
complaints, harmful overuse, extreme spend concentration, or blocked exit.

## 6. Experiment protocol

1. Complete the label-neutral truth ledger and safety screen.
2. Define the customer value that payment is meant to exchange.
3. Change one price, presentation, timing, or entitlement property while holding
   delivered value and eligibility rules constant where possible.
4. Use a meaningful control and predefined adult, non-vulnerable population.
5. Measure net revenue and one customer outcome plus refund, complaint, regret,
   exit, and harmful-spend guardrails.
6. Use a horizon long enough to observe renewal, retention, refund, and support
   effects.
7. Reject a treatment whose short-term revenue gain fails a guardrail.

Use conjoint or Best–Worst Scaling to study tradeoffs and proposition priority;
use randomized experiments for causal campaign or flow effects. See
[research-methods.md](research-methods.md).

## 7. Vitia integration

- **Avaritia:** total value, price comparison, ownership, and fair risk reversal.
- **Gula:** repeat purchase, depletion, collection, satiation, and overuse risk.
- **Acedia:** payment friction, cancellation, cost comprehension, and recovery.
- **Ira:** price fairness, blocked goals, complaint, and procedural justice.
- **Invidia:** competitive or social purchase pressure and reference groups.
- **Superbia:** cosmetic identity and earned distinction without status shame.
- **Luxuria:** vivid presentation and immediate experience without obscuring cost.

These are post-evidence routing tags. Never decide that a transaction is
“Avaritia” because it involves money or “Gula” because it repeats.

## 8. Source boundary

The practitioner framing comes from the Notion lesson
[#21. お金の話](https://app.notion.com/p/26739cbfbab980cbb2fadca1f0e98cd3),
which connects UX with monetization and explicitly frames the lesson as learning
how money is taken so participants can avoid exploitation. Vitia converts that
framing into a protection-first, model-neutral audit rather than instructions to
exploit cognitive or neurological “bugs.”
